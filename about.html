ABOUT ME

Language is more than communication. It's how we understand people.

I'm Divya Kadav, a linguist, German Language Specialist and translator interested in the intersection of language, research and technology.

MY STORY

From curiosity to linguistics.

I am a Linguist and German Language Specialist with a strong academic foundation in linguistics, translation, and multilingual communication.

My work combines language expertise with research, technology, and cultural understanding, allowing me to approach language not only as a means of communication but also as a system that can be analyzed, documented, and understood.

The question that started it all

My journey into linguistics began with a simple question: Why do children across the world often use similar sounds when referring to their parents?

Across many unrelated languages, words for "mother" and "father" frequently contain sounds such as /m/, /p/, /b/, and /f/. Whether it is mother and father in English, maa and papa in Hindi, eomma and appa in Korean, mama and papa in Japanese, māma and bàba in Mandarin Chinese, mama and papa in Russian, mamá and papá in Spanish, or mama and baba in Arabic, I was fascinated by how languages separated by geography, culture, and history could share such similar sound patterns.

As I explored this question further, I learned that these sounds are among the earliest and easiest for infants to produce. This discovery introduced me to the field of phonology and sparked a deeper curiosity about how human language develops, evolves, and is organized.

What began as a simple observation gradually became a passion that led me to pursue a Master's degree in Linguistics.

Learning languages, studying language

Alongside my linguistics studies, I was also learning Japanese and later German. Japanese, in particular, deepened my interest in language structure through its mora-based rhythm system, which differs significantly from the syllable-based systems found in many other languages.

Understanding how languages can organize sound in fundamentally different yet systematic ways strengthened my desire to study language scientifically rather than simply learn it.

Research & Language Technology

My academic background includes research in language documentation, phonetics, morphology, translation studies, and computational linguistics.

I have worked on projects involving low-resource language documentation, corpus annotation, IPA transcription, machine translation evaluation, and linguistic data analysis.

Through field research, I collected and analyzed over 50 hours of primary speech data, contributing to the documentation of underrepresented language varieties.

A Multilingual Perspective

In addition to German, I have studied Japanese, Mandarin Chinese, Hindi, English, Marathi, and Indian Sign Language.

This multilingual experience has given me a broader perspective on how languages function and interact across different cultures and communities.

My experience spans translation, research, documentation, proofreading, content development, and academic coordination, enabling me to work effectively in both language-focused and interdisciplinary environments.

Where I'm Heading

Today, my interests lie at the intersection of linguistics, translation, localization, language documentation, and language technology.

I am particularly interested in Natural Language Processing (NLP), machine translation, corpus linguistics, multilingual communication, and the ways technology can support language preservation and accessibility.

My goal is to contribute to projects that bridge language, technology, and global communication while supporting accurate, accessible, and culturally informed language solutions.
